Task 1Line graphBand 7.5

The graph below shows the number of visitors to three museums in a European city between 2000 and 2020. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

The line graph illustrates how many people visited three museums in a European city over a twenty-year period, from 2000 to 2020.

Overall, while visitor numbers at Museum A rose consistently throughout the period, Museum C experienced a steady decline. Museum B, by contrast, remained comparatively stable, fluctuating only slightly around the same level.

In 2000, Museum C was the most popular of the three, attracting approximately 600,000 visitors, almost three times as many as Museum A, which started at just 200,000. Over the following two decades, however, this gap narrowed considerably and eventually reversed. Museum A saw a sharp and continuous rise, climbing to around 500,000 visitors by 2010 before reaching a peak of 850,000 in 2020, making it by far the most visited attraction by the end of the period.

Museum C followed the opposite trajectory, dropping gradually to roughly 350,000 by 2010 and falling further to just 150,000 in 2020, a decrease of 450,000 from its starting figure. Meanwhile, Museum B hovered around the 400,000 mark for most of the period, dipping slightly in the middle years before recovering to finish close to where it began.

Vì sao đạt 7.5: Có overview tách biệt nêu đúng 2 xu hướng chính; số liệu được nhóm theo đặc điểm (tăng/giảm/ổn định) thay vì liệt kê theo thời gian; dùng đa dạng từ vựng chỉ xu hướng (rose, climbing, dropping, hovered, dipping) và cấu trúc so sánh (almost three times as many as, by far the most visited).
Task 1Pie chartBand 7.0

The charts below show the proportion of household spending in a European country in 1980 and 2020. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

The two pie charts compare how households in a European country allocated their spending across several categories in 1980 and 2020.

Overall, spending on housing and leisure increased considerably as a proportion of the total budget over the forty-year period, whereas the share spent on food fell sharply. Housing remained the largest single category in both years.

In 1980, food accounted for the second-largest proportion of household spending, at 30%, only slightly behind housing, which made up 22% of the total. By 2020, this pattern had changed dramatically: housing had risen to 35%, becoming the dominant expense, while spending on food had dropped to just 15%, less than half its original share.

Leisure activities also saw a notable rise, doubling from 10% to 20% and overtaking food to become the second-largest category by 2020. The remaining categories, including clothing and transport, together made up a comparatively small and fairly stable portion of the budget in both years, with only minor fluctuations of two or three percentage points.

Vì sao đạt 7.0: Overview nêu đúng 2 xu hướng nổi bật nhất và giữ nguyên tắc "không có số liệu trong overview"; các đối tượng được so sánh chéo giữa 2 năm (housing vs food) thay vì mô tả từng biểu đồ riêng lẻ. Để lên 7.5+ cần thêm 1-2 cụm từ ít phổ biến hơn cho phần liên hệ giữa các mục nhỏ còn lại.
Task 1Process diagramBand 7.5

The diagram below shows the process of recycling plastic bottles. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

The diagram illustrates the sequence of stages involved in recycling used plastic bottles, from initial collection to the production of reusable plastic pellets.

Overall, the process consists of six distinct stages and follows a linear, one-directional path, with each stage transforming the material further towards its final reusable form.

The process begins with the collection of used plastic bottles, which are typically gathered from household recycling bins and public collection points. Once collected, the bottles are transported to a recycling facility, where they are sorted according to type and colour. Following this sorting stage, the plastic is thoroughly cleaned to remove any labels, residue or contaminants that might affect the quality of the final product.

After cleaning, the plastic is fed into a machine and shredded into small flakes. These flakes are then melted at high temperatures, a step which allows the material to be reshaped. Finally, the melted plastic is extruded and cut into small pellets, which can subsequently be sold to manufacturers and used as raw material for producing new plastic goods.

Vì sao đạt 7.5: Dùng câu bị động xuyên suốt (are sorted, is cleaned, is fed, are cut) phù hợp với thể loại process; từ nối trình tự đa dạng (Once collected, Following this, After cleaning, Finally) thay vì lặp "first, second, third"; overview nêu rõ số bước và tính chất tuyến tính của quy trình.
Task 2OpinionBand 7.5

Some people believe that children should begin learning a foreign language as soon as they start primary school, rather than waiting until secondary school. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

It is often suggested that foreign language education should start in primary school rather than being delayed until secondary level. I strongly agree with this view, primarily because of how the brain acquires language at a young age and the long-term academic advantages this provides.

To begin with, young children possess a remarkable capacity for absorbing new sounds and grammatical patterns, a cognitive advantage that diminishes significantly with age. Neuroscientific research has consistently shown that the brain's plasticity for language acquisition is at its highest before puberty, allowing children to develop near-native pronunciation and intuitive grammar with relatively little conscious effort. A child who begins studying French at the age of six, for instance, is far more likely to develop an authentic accent than a teenager starting the same course at fifteen, simply because the younger learner's brain is still highly receptive to new phonetic patterns.

Furthermore, early exposure to a second language brings cognitive benefits that extend well beyond linguistic competence. Studies in bilingual education suggest that children who learn a foreign language from an early age tend to develop stronger problem-solving skills and greater mental flexibility, since they are constantly required to switch between two different linguistic systems. This cognitive training can, in turn, have a positive knock-on effect on other academic subjects, including mathematics and reading comprehension, as children become more adept at recognising patterns and structures.

In conclusion, given the clear neurological advantages of early language learning and its broader cognitive benefits, I firmly believe that foreign languages should be introduced from the very first years of primary school rather than postponed until secondary education.

Vì sao đạt 7.5: Thesis statement rõ ràng ngay từ mở bài; mỗi thân bài phát triển SÂU 1 ý (giải thích cơ chế + ví dụ cụ thể) thay vì liệt kê nhiều ý hời hợt; dùng cấu trúc câu đa dạng (mệnh đề quan hệ, so sánh, mệnh đề trạng ngữ); từ vựng học thuật tự nhiên (cognitive plasticity, knock-on effect, phonetic patterns).
Task 2DiscussionBand 7.0

Some people think that the best way to reduce crime is to give longer prison sentences. Others, however, believe there are better alternative ways to reduce crime. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

Crime remains a pressing social issue, and opinions differ sharply on how best to address it. While some argue that lengthening prison sentences is the most effective deterrent, others contend that alternative measures would yield better long-term results. This essay will examine both perspectives before presenting my own view.

Proponents of harsher sentencing argue that the fear of spending many years behind bars discourages potential offenders from committing crimes in the first place. From this perspective, a strict justice system sends a clear message that criminal behaviour carries severe consequences, thereby protecting society by keeping dangerous individuals off the streets for longer periods. Countries that have adopted tougher sentencing policies, for example, often cite short-term reductions in specific violent crimes as evidence of this deterrent effect.

On the other hand, many experts maintain that focusing solely on punishment fails to address the root causes of criminal behaviour, such as poverty, lack of education and limited employment opportunities. They argue that investing in rehabilitation programmes, vocational training and mental health support within prisons is far more likely to prevent reoffending than simply extending sentences. Indeed, some countries with strong rehabilitation-focused systems report considerably lower rates of repeat offending than those relying primarily on punitive measures.

Having considered both sides, I believe that a combined approach is most effective. While sentences should be proportionate to the severity of the crime, greater emphasis should be placed on rehabilitation, since addressing the underlying causes of crime is ultimately more likely to create a safer society in the long run.

Task 2Advantages/DisadvantagesBand 7.0

In many countries, more and more people are choosing to work from home. Do the advantages of this trend outweigh the disadvantages?

Remote working has become increasingly common in recent years, largely driven by advances in digital technology. While this shift brings clear benefits to employees, it also introduces certain drawbacks. In my view, however, the advantages of working from home outweigh the disadvantages for most people.

One of the most significant benefits of remote work is the flexibility it offers. Employees no longer need to spend hours commuting, which not only saves valuable time but also reduces stress and transport costs. This additional time can be redirected towards personal wellbeing, family responsibilities or professional development, ultimately leading to a healthier work-life balance. Moreover, many workers report higher productivity when working from home, as they are able to design their own schedules around their most productive hours, free from the constant interruptions typical of a busy office environment.

Nevertheless, working from home is not without its downsides. Isolation is a common complaint among remote workers, who may miss the spontaneous social interaction and sense of camaraderie that an office provides. This lack of face-to-face contact can, over time, negatively affect mental health and reduce opportunities for collaborative brainstorming. In addition, the boundary between work and personal life can become blurred, leading some employees to work longer hours than they would in a traditional office setting.

Despite these concerns, I would argue that the benefits of improved flexibility, reduced commuting stress and greater autonomy generally outweigh the challenges, particularly when companies actively support remote employees through regular virtual check-ins and clear boundaries around working hours.

Để lên band cao hơn, nên thêm ví dụ/số liệu thực tế minh họa cho luận điểm về năng suất.
Task 2Problem-SolutionBand 7.5

Traffic congestion is becoming a serious problem in many major cities. What are the causes of this problem, and what solutions can you suggest?

Traffic congestion has become an increasingly severe problem in cities around the world, causing significant economic losses and environmental damage. This essay will explore the main causes of this issue before proposing some practical solutions.

The primary cause of traffic congestion is the rapid growth in private car ownership, driven largely by rising incomes and inadequate public transport infrastructure. As more households can afford a car, and as buses and trains remain overcrowded, unreliable or simply absent in many areas, individuals naturally turn to private vehicles as their preferred mode of transport. This is compounded by urban planning that has historically prioritised road-building over the development of efficient public transit networks, creating cities that are, in effect, designed around the car rather than the pedestrian or commuter.

To address this problem, city authorities should prioritise substantial investment in public transportation systems, ensuring that buses and trains are frequent, affordable and able to reach even the outer suburbs. If commuters had access to a reliable alternative, many would willingly abandon their cars for daily journeys, particularly if combined with congestion charges that make driving into city centres financially unattractive during peak hours. Singapore's electronic road pricing system, for example, has demonstrated that charging drivers for entering congested zones can meaningfully reduce traffic volumes while simultaneously generating revenue that can be reinvested in public transport.

In conclusion, while traffic congestion stems largely from over-reliance on private vehicles and insufficient public transport, a combination of infrastructure investment and financial disincentives for driving offers a realistic path towards more manageable city traffic.

Task 2Two-part questionBand 7.0

In many countries, the amount of food wasted by households and restaurants is increasing every year. Why is this happening? Is it a positive or negative development?

Food waste has been rising steadily in many parts of the world, and this essay will consider why this trend is occurring before evaluating whether it represents a positive or negative development for society.

One major reason behind growing food waste is the relative affordability and abundance of food in many modern economies. When food is cheap and readily available, consumers tend to place less value on it, buying more than they need and discarding items that are still edible simply because they have passed a "best before" date. Restaurants, too, often over-prepare dishes to ensure they never run out of menu items, resulting in significant quantities of unused food being thrown away at the end of each day. This behaviour is further encouraged by supermarket promotions that incentivise bulk buying, regardless of whether households can realistically consume everything before it spoils.

In my view, this is an overwhelmingly negative development. Beyond the obvious ethical concern of discarding edible food while millions of people worldwide suffer from hunger, food waste carries substantial environmental costs. Decomposing food in landfills releases methane, a greenhouse gas far more potent than carbon dioxide, thereby contributing directly to climate change. Moreover, the resources used to produce wasted food, including water, land and labour, are effectively squandered, representing an enormous inefficiency in the global food system.

In conclusion, rising food waste is driven mainly by the low value placed on cheap, abundant food, and given its serious environmental and ethical consequences, it should be regarded as a clearly negative trend that requires urgent action.
